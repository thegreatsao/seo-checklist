### The viewport, declared before the change

StatCounter, mobile, worldwide, September 2025 to September 2026 (`tools/calibration/mobile-viewport.json`,
written by `tools/calibrate_mobile_viewport.py`): no listed width is under 360; 360 holds 25.7% of the listed
share; 48.9% of it is narrower than 390. A fixed `width`/`min-width` between 361 and 390 CSS pixels therefore
overflows a quarter of listed phones and passed MB-100. Rule: the narrowest width the list holds. **Limitation**:
the rule was written after 390 had been checked against the same list; the list folds about a third of its share
into "Other"; a `screen` width is the layout viewport only with `width=device-width`, which MB-100 checks first.

Expected, declared before running: MB-100 on `<div style="width: 380px">` — `PASS` before, `FAIL` after; on
`width: 360px` — `PASS` both. No other item reads this number.

### Lighthouse and the one-week cache line

Lighthouse's `uses-long-cache-ttl` (v12.0.0 source) models a return visit's cache age from Chrome's deciles
(`[0, 0.2, 1, 3, 8, 12, 24, 48, 72, 168, 8760]` hours): one week is the 90th percentile, and Lighthouse flags an
asset until the modelled hit rate passes 92.5%, about 96 days. So the tree's week fails "short or missing", not
"less than optimal"; adopting Lighthouse's line would fail every site caching assets between a week and three
months. Written into the basis as a choice, not a standard; open to Anton.
