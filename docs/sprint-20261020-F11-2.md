## Scope

Twenty-eight `inherited` numbers whose value is a definition or a prioritisation (four, to `convention`), or that
feed only a field no registry item reads at the severity the number writes (twenty-four, to `presentation`). For
each: what it does, why no corpus would settle it, what changes if it moves. A number that decides a verdict and
that a corpus could settle is not here (batch 3); the nine Search Console numbers, `HIGH_SEVERITY_IMPRESSIONS`
among them, go with their shared known-issues entry in batch 4; `detect_profile`'s tables go to batch 3, because
under `--profile auto` they decide which items are N/A and a labelled set of homepages could measure the detector.

How each `presentation` claim was found: every item over the script was listed with its `assert`, `warn` and
`applies_when` paths (`checklist.json`), each constant's uses were traced to the output field it writes, and the
severity it writes was read through `SEVERITY_ALIAS` (`warning` → medium, `info` → low) against each
`none_severity` list. Found on the way and **kept out of this batch** because they do decide a verdict:
`MIN_PARAGRAPH_WORDS` (the paragraphs it drops are KW-076's body text), `COLLECTION_THIN_WORDS` (a `warning`
AR-154 counts), the other five `llms_txt_checker` numbers (bonuses in GEO-002's `quality.score`),
`PATH_EXPLOSION_VARIANTS` (a `warning` AR-163 counts). Found and **not fixed** (a follow-up): TE-179 decides on a
`90` written in its registry rule, beside `YOUNG_DOMAIN_DAYS`; the scan cannot see a registry literal.
