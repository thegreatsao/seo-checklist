## Scope

- `audit_reachability.observed_failures()`: every script-backed item the recorded census (`tests/census.json`) saw
  report `FAIL` on at least one served tree. The census is a run of the runner with the registry's own arguments, and
  its CI job and `ci_local` re-derive it from five audits on every run, so the claim is earned again each time. Three
  refusals, each a way a recorded `FAIL` would not be the assertion's:
  - **a census of another registry** (`registry_version` differs) claims nothing: its rows describe other rules;
  - **an item carrying `entry_answer`** is not claimed: the runner's entry gate writes its `FAIL` without the
    assertion (CI-001, CI-003, CI-015, TE-167 — plan group B, batch 2);
  - **an item in `tests/harness.py`'s `CLOCK_READ`** is not claimed: the census check compares its answer only as
    "timed" (`verdict_census.as_held`), so a recorded `FAIL` there is the recording machine's and no gate re-derives it
    (TECH-003). Read from the harness file as data — importing `harness` arms the tripwire.
- `audit()`: a rule proved unable to report FAIL **and** seen failing is a finding, kind `contradicted` — one of the
  two is wrong, and it is never the census. This widens `NothingTheFixturesHaveFailedIsCalledUnfailable` from the
  oracle's declarations to every recorded answer.
- The summary prints the claim: `N seen to report FAIL in the recorded census …`, and the unclaimed count becomes the
  items neither proved nor seen. `ESTABLISHES` / `DOES_NOT_ESTABLISH` and the module docstring say what the new claim
  rests on and what it does not establish.
- `tests/test_prose_counts.py`: `assertion_counts()` gains `seen`; README, ROADMAP and KNOWN-ISSUES sentences move to
  the new counts, with a held claim for the seen count.
- REG-10 (`openspec/specs/registry/spec.md`): two scenarios and the Reader line.
