Every loaf on the counter today, in the order they came out of the oven. We bake in small batches from four in the morning, so the first loaves are cool by the time the door opens and the last are still warm at noon. The rye is a three-day sourdough with a dark crust and a close crumb; the spelt is lighter and sweeter, and keeps for most of a week wrapped in a cloth. The sourdough is our everyday loaf, an open crumb and a crust that crackles as it cools on the rack. The brioche is made with butter and eggs from a farm two villages away and is best on the day it is baked, toasted the day after.

Everything here is baked on the premises. Ask at the counter if you want a loaf sliced, and tell us the day before if you need more than four of anything, because the ovens hold only so many and the dough needs its night in the cold to develop flavour.
